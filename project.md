# Ethical Reflection & Improvements 

In today's age, where the world has become digitalised, information is constantly being collected, processed and shared. There is a need for strong data protection rules. Do users really know exactly how much of their personal data is being collected every day? As social media evolved, platforms began using personal data to personalise content, recommend posts and display targeted advertisements. Many applications such as Tiktok, Instagram and Snapchat do this. Data protection is a fundamental right under EU law. EU data protection legislation consists of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which came into effect on May 25, 2018, the Law Enforcement Directive (LED), and the Data Protection Regulation for EU bodies, offices and institutions. GDPR means that remarketing on social media is prohibited unless a user has agreed to such activity. Users must also agree to privacy policy. Ethically, the main concern is that users may not neccessarily know exctly how their online activity is collected. Personal information can reveal peoples preferences. Social media companies can improve by Making privacy policies easier to understand because most people may find it quite confusing at first glance. They should also only collect the personal data necessary for a specific purpose. Some apps use personal information that isn't neccesary and don't explain exactly why it's being used. Social media apps should be able to explain to users exactly why their personal information is required.
