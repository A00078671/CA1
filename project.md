These changes have transformed the way people around the world communicate with each other, share information and view content.

Today's algorithm-driven social media platforms help businesses and communities grow but it also made it easier for cyberbullying and phishing to spread. So, their overall impact depends on how individuals and businesses use them.

# Connection and community

Early platforms like Myspace were built around profiles. Users could only customise their page, add friends and leave comments on each other's profiles but what they saw came mostly from people they already knew. Now that there are algorithm-driven platforms like TikTok, Instagram and improved Facebook people get recommended content and people based on interests so users can find communities they would have never met otherwise and stay close to friends. For example, niche hobby groups, support communities and people in other countries.

Pew Research Center (2015) found out that 57% of U.S. teens had made new friends online with common interests, mostly in social media platforms like Facebook and Instagram. About 64% of those who had met a new friend online said that met new friends through a social platforms. Furthermore, Pew Research Center (2018) also found that 77% of teens say that they spend a lot of time in online groups and forums. Most of them also said that these sites help people their age interact with individuals from different backgrounds. Thus, social media platforms help young people meet new people, find groups and connect with others beyond people they already know.

# Ethical Reflection & Improvements 

In today's age, where the world has become digitalised, information is constantly being collected, processed and shared. There is a need for strong data protection rules. Do users really know exactly how much of their personal data is being collected every day? As social media evolved, platforms began using personal data to personalise content, recommend posts and display targeted advertisements. Many applications such as Tiktok, Instagram and Snapchat do this. Data protection is a fundamental right under EU law. EU data protection legislation consists of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which came into effect on May 25, 2018, the Law Enforcement Directive (LED), and the Data Protection Regulation for EU bodies, offices and institutions. GDPR means that remarketing on social media is prohibited unless a user has agreed to such activity. Users must also agree to privacy policy. Ethically, the main concern is that users may not neccessarily know exctly how their online activity is collected. Personal information can reveal peoples preferences. Social media companies can improve by Making privacy policies easier to understand because most people may find it quite confusing at first glance. They should also only collect the personal data necessary for a specific purpose. Some apps use personal information that isn't neccesary and don't explain exactly why it's being used. Social media apps should be able to explain to users exactly why their personal information is required.
