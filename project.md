# Historical Development



In 1969 - The first message "lo" is sent over the ARPANET (they were trying type the word "login" but after "lo" it crashed). ARPANET was established in the late 1960s by the ARPR, now known as DARPA, which is the world's direct technical precursor to the modern internet.

1973 - CM (Community Memory) was the first computerized bulletin board system (computer sever running software that allow users to connect to the system using a terminal or a terminal emulator.)

1979-1980 - Usenet (Use's Network) was conceived and established. Users read and post message to one or more topic categories, known as newsgroup's.

The first social media platform ever made by definition were Six Degrees and Friendster neither of these platforms are around anymore despite their significance in the building of social media.

In 1997 six Degrees was launched while the company who made it Macro View was founded in 1966 by Andrew Weinreich. Friendster was created by Jonathan Abrams and was launched in 2002.

Big names in social media such as Facebook launched in 2004 going live in 2006 and Myspace launched in the early 2003 all used relatively the same idea with some tweaks being made and some new additions such as stories and voice messages. They all have one thing in common even from the very start. Allow people to communicate and share information as efficiently as possible.

2005 - YouTube launched and started monetizing their content with add revenue. This was a breakthrough for the development of social media as during 2007 YouTube introduced collaborations, partnership programme and it allowed more people to connect. It was also the first platform to pay influencers and content creators based on their content, views and followers, allowing them to peruse content creation as a full-time job.

2006 - Facebook launched its News Feed and by 2010 it used algorithms called EdgeRank to choose what stories appeared in each user's feed.

2010 - Instagram launched, it attracted users as it allowed them to take photos and videos with different filters, it connected people through comments and likes. It became popular in 2013 as it only allowed a short 2 to 3 min videos unlike YouTube that had longer videos, these videos kept viewers more engaged.

2011 - MySpace was sold because it lost a lot of members to Facebook. 

2012 - Facebook bought Instagram and reached 1 billion active users.

2016 - Instagram announced that its feed will show the moments it believed each user would care the most about instead of the most recent posts. In the same year ByteDance launched Douyin in China and in 2017 - TikTok launched outside mainland China.

2018 - Musical.ly was merged into TikTok. TikTok's For You feed recommends videos based on how users interact with content. For example, what they like, comment on, search for.

2007-2026 - Since the smartphone penetration rate is getting higher and social media like TikTok, snapchat and Instagram had launched, social media became parts of everyday communication.

## Societal Viewpoint Analysis 1
# Defining the social viewpoint

The societal viewpoint looks at how the evolution of social media platforms has affected communication, relationships, communities, individuals and wellbeing.

# Positives
# 1
# Connection and community

Early platforms like Myspace were built around profiles. Users could only customise their page, add friends and leave comments on each other's profiles but what they saw came mostly from people they already knew. Now that there are algorithm-driven platforms like TikTok, Instagram and improved Facebook people get recommended content and people based on interests so users can find communities they would have never met otherwise and stay close to friends. For example, niche hobby groups, support communities and people in other countries.

Pew Research Center (2015) found out that 57% of U.S. teens had made new friends online with common interests, mostly in social media platforms like Facebook and Instagram. About 64% of those who had met a new friend online said that met new friends through a social platforms. Furthermore, Pew Research Center (2018) also found that 77% of teens say that they spend a lot of time in online groups and forums. Most of them also said that these sites help people their age interact with individuals from different backgrounds. Thus, social media platforms help young people meet new people, find groups and connect with others beyond people they already know.

# 2
# Educational benefits

The evolution of social media platform has significantly expanded the access to knowledge and information worldwide, due to social media people from all around the world have access to educational material, unlike the traditional way of using textbooks and library resources. Students can access tutorials and basic knowledge about their desired topic through platforms like Instagram and TikTok, for example: Studyclix, is an Ireland based Leaving Cert website that has past exam papers and helps students with revision, due to social media they are able to post videos and interact with students better and are able to advertise about their grins etc.

Unlike traditional learning where students are relied of textbooks and their notes, social media has made learning more visual and fun. Platforms like YouTube and Instagram allows educational topics to be presented in a video form, animated, demonstration by using graphs etc. Research by Perez et al. (2023) shows that these visual aspects allow students to understand the topic better and makes learning more fun and engaging also bringing out the creative side of students.



# Negatives
# Privacy 

Early profile-based social platforms mainly showed what users chose to put on their own page and share with people they are friends with. But now algorithm-driven platforms personalise what individuals see by collecting information about what users do, share, like, follow and interact with (TikTok, 2020). For people that use such platforms means that they have less control over their own personal data. For example, the Cambridge Analytica scandal in 2018, where the data firm gathered details of around 87 million Facebook users without their permission. The US Federal Trade Commission found out that Facebook's misleading privacy disclosures let it share users' personal information with third-party apps even though the users had not given them permission to do so. This example shows that personalisation brings real privacy risks for everyone.

# 2
# Cyberbullying

The evolution of social media has increased the opportunities of cyberbullying. This has various side effects for people who are going through this, cyberbullying may affect the individual mentally and can cause severe depression and social anxiety. Due to platforms like Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, bullying has become more accessible as the person bullying can be anonymous and they don't need to show their faces. According to Cybersafe kids (2024) report 38% of 12-14-year-olds have experienced cyberbullying and have mostly kept it to themselves. These experiences can cause students to have lack of confidence and may get distracted at school. This is also common to see in the comment section of celebrities and influencers, people who have a different opinion or dislike these celebrities are also likely to say something hurtful towards them. Sometimes this can lead to harassment and body shaming.

Due to cases like these the EU government had enforced law such as Digital Service Act (DSA), which sets obligations of these platforms and to address illegal posting and manage online risks. For example: Instagram, it filters and hides hateful or potential harmful comments. These safety measures taken help in creating social media into a more positive and safe place.

# Ethical Reflection & Improvements 

In today's age, where the world has become digitalised, information is constantly being collected, processed and shared. There is a need for strong data protection rules. Do users really know exactly how much of their personal data is being collected every day? As social media evolved, platforms began using personal data to personalise content, recommend posts and display targeted advertisements. Many applications such as TikTok, Instagram and Snapchat do this. Data protection is a fundamental right under EU law. EU data protection legislation consists of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which came into effect on May 25, 2018, the Law Enforcement Directive (LED), and the Data Protection Regulation for EU bodies, offices and institutions. GDPR means that remarketing on social media is prohibited unless a user has agreed to such activity. 

Users must also agree to privacy policy. Ethically, the main concern is that users may not necessarily know exactly how their online activity is collected. Personal information can reveal people,s preferences. Social media companies can improve by Making privacy policies easier to understand because most people may find it quite confusing at first glance. They should also only collect the personal data necessary for a specific purpose. Some apps use personal information that isn't necessary and don't explain exactly why it's being used. Social media apps should be able to explain to users exactly why their personal information is required.
